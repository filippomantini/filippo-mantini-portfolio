THE CONCEPT

A stop on Milan’s red metro line.
Destination: COLLAPSE.

On 26 April 2025, Collapse Private Party at Formula Discoteque in Città di Castello took the form of an imaginary stop: PZ. COLLAPSE. With Dukwa and Bannyblanco, the language of the metro connected communication, entry objects and the venue.

MY ROLE

From the idea
to the room.

I managed the entire project: organisation, PR and DJs, visual identity and posts, the design and ordering of merchandise and tickets, through to building and installing the venue setup.

01 / VISUAL IDENTITY

The language
of the red line.

Red and white, oversized lettering and visible composition grids: the identity draws on the language of the metro and brings it into Collapse Private Party.

The communication moves between digital formats and physical objects. Posts and stories announce the night; presales, cards and signs accompany guests into the room.

03 / PRESALE & ENTRY

The ticket becomes
a metro pass.

Two distinct objects, two moments in the experience: the paper presale before the night and the card handed out at the entrance.

The card, resembling a metro pass, was scanned to access the room. The act of entering was part of the concept.

01

The presale.

The paper presale introduced the night’s experience: the first physical element of the identity, before arriving at the venue.

02

The card.

At the entrance, each customer received a card resembling a metro pass. The theme became an object to use.

03

Entering the room.

The card was scanned at the entrance to the room to gain access. This final step completed the reference to travelling on the metro.

NFC / PZ. COLLAPSE

The card
in use.

The original video shows the card being read via NFC: the reference to a metro pass becomes part of the event entry system.

04 / SETUP & WAYFINDING

The stop
takes shape.

I built and installed the identity elements inside the room. The metro stop reference became an environment to move through, as well as an image to see.

I designed three signs to be printed and hung inside the venue to guide guests. Communication and wayfinding shared the same visual language.
